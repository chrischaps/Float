# Float — vision

You are adrift. The raft is small and honest — logs you lashed yourself —
and the ocean is enormous and indifferent. Nothing is asked of you. You can
look at your feet in the water, watch the light change, listen.

## Pillars

1. **The ocean is the protagonist.** Wave motion must be believable —
   real dispersion, real steepness — because your body reads the deck's
   hesitation before it follows a swell. Everything else can be a drawing;
   the physics cannot.
2. **Stylized, not photoreal.** Flat bold blues, hard-edged graphic foam,
   pastel skies (see reference/). Cel bands, not gradients. The water is
   a woodblock print that moves like the sea.
3. **Time is the drama.** Sunrise → noon glare → red sunset → violet dusk →
   starscape. One continuous parameter, no cuts. A day takes ~10 minutes;
   nothing else "happens."
4. **Quiet interactivity.** Look around. (Later: scoot along the edge,
   around corners.) The absence of goals is the point.

## Feel targets

- Sitting through one full day cycle should feel like a rest, not a wait.
- Looking down at your legs refracting in the water should produce the
  small shock of embodiment — "those are mine."
- At night the foam catches starlight and the horizon disappears.

## Later

Scooting, ambient music, a distant whale breach at dusk (once, rare),
dawn birds, bioluminescent night foam.
